===== OVERVIEW (Description) =====

Save a video, an audio file or a download from a web page to your computer. Everything is free, nothing is uploaded, and there is nothing else to install.

Open the extension on a page that is playing something and it lists what it found. Pick one, or tick several and take them all at once. Downloads keep going in a tab of their own: interrupt one, close the tab, restart the browser, and it carries on from where it stopped instead of starting over.

WHAT IT DOES

• Finds the video, audio, images and files a page loads, on the sites you turn it on for
• Resumes an interrupted download instead of restarting it
• Downloads in parallel over several connections, so a big file finishes sooner
• Queues as many as you like and works through them while you do something else
• Checks every finished file with a SHA-256 digest read back off the disk, so you know the copy is intact
• Turns a stream into one playable MP4, joining its pieces without re-encoding anything
• Saves just the audio as an M4A or MP3
• Pulls subtitles out of a stream as SRT or WebVTT, and can translate them using your browser's own translator
• Converts video and audio between MP4, WebM, M4A, MP3, WAV and OGG
• Repairs a stalled download by joining leftover .ts pieces back into one file

FREE MEANS FREE

Every feature above is free and always will be. There is no account, no sign-in, no trial, no credits, no daily limit, and no paid tier holding anything back. There is nothing to upgrade to.

Nothing you download is sent anywhere. There is no analytics, no tracking, and no advertising. The extension asks for access one site at a time, and until you turn it on for a site it sees nothing that happens there.

There is also no companion program. Some downloaders ask you to install a separate helper application before their main features work; this one does everything inside the browser, so there is no installer, no background service, and nothing left behind if you remove it.

WHAT IT DELIBERATELY WILL NOT DO

It downloads open web media only. Sites whose terms of service or copy protection forbid downloading are refused, and that refusal is written into the code rather than a policy page — there is no setting that turns it off. Encrypted streams are refused outright: no key is ever fetched and nothing is ever decrypted. Live streams are refused because they have no end to download to.

If that is not the tool you are looking for, this is not the tool you are looking for, and it is better to know now.

OPEN SOURCE

The whole thing is open source under MIT or Apache-2.0, and the engine that decides what to fetch and how to rebuild it is written in Rust. You can read exactly what it does, including the refusals above:

https://github.com/opentoolkitsg/opendownloader

There is also a website that does the same job for a link you paste, for when you cannot install an extension.
